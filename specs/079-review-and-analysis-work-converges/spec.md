# 079 — Review and analysis work converges

A spec's `/{project}:review` and `/{project}:analyze` work ends in a bounded number of runs, and one spec's run does not create work for another spec. This is the efficiency work `078-review-fix-rounds-are-re-read-within-a-bound` was meant to be.

## Motivation

`078-review-fix-rounds-are-re-read-within-a-bound` bounded one loop: the fix rounds inside a single `/{project}:review` run, re-read by an independent agent within a budget of two re-reads. That loop worked; 078's second review ran two rounds and one re-read. The cost the owner was losing time to was the outer loop: a review deferred part of what it found into the spec it reviewed, or into a sibling, so another implement → review → analyze cycle followed, and that review found more. No criterion of 078 measured an outcome, so its reaching `done` could not have shown whether reviews converged.

The corpus showed it. Each figure was measured on 2026-11-04 at `d62e72f3`, before this spec existed:

- **Status.** 37 specs were `in-progress`, 34 `done` and 5 `draft`, read from each `specs/NNN-*/spec.md`'s `status:`. 10 of the 37 had no unchecked `- [ ]` line in their `tasks.md`, so they waited only on review, analysis or the completion gate: 015, 016, 029, 034, 037, 039, 045, 055, 061 and 078. Eight more had exactly one.
- **Runs per spec.** Counted from commit subjects that start `review(NNN)` or `analyze(NNN)`, over all history; a run recorded under another subject, or two runs in one commit, are not counted.
  - `done` specs: review runs median 2, maximum 7, and 11 of 34 took more than two (006, 013, 019, 023, 025, 040, 042, 043, 046, 051, 062). Analyze runs median 1, maximum 5.
  - `in-progress` specs: review runs median 2, maximum 52 (022), and 15 of 37 had already taken more than two (008, 017, 020, 022, 026, 029, 036, 041, 045, 047, 048, 050, 052, 058, 063). Analyze runs median 2, maximum 21.
- **Share of the history.** Since 2026-10-01, 112 of 847 commits were `review(` or `analyze(` commits, by subject prefix.
- **078 itself.** It was drafted on 2026-11-03 with nine tasks; its first review (`f47d2b03`) added five more (tasks 10–14) and recorded 40 observations; its second (`d62e72f3`) found 4 violations and 41 observations in its passes. Its artifacts and code came to 3,820 lines (`wc -l` over `git ls-files` of its spec directory, its five loop primitives and `runtime/tests/fix_rounds.rs`).

078's second review showed where the outer loop came from:

- **The window.** The default diff base, the parent of 078's `in-progress` commit, resolved 127 scope entries; a base at the first review's commit resolved 62 (`compute-review-scope`). Of those 62, 27 were touched by 078's own commits since the first review and 28 only by 066's commits (`git diff-tree` per commit), and 17 of the 45 items the passes found (2 violations, 15 observations) concerned 066's or 026's work, as 078's `review.md` records them.
- **Copies drifting.** 16 of the 21 observations fixed as a group had prose-only fixes: doc comments, MCP descriptions, the changelog, the command, the reference, and 078's spec, plan and data model disagreeing with the code or with each other (classified by whether the fix edited only prose). All 7 findings of its one re-read were prose too.
- **Deferral into the reviewed spec.** Every deferral to 078's own tasks guaranteed another cycle. The owner then chose to waive, under the AGENTS.md rule that waives an observation about the pipeline's own machinery outside the code under review, 14 observations that the recommendations had proposed deferring.
- **The tie-breaker.** AGENTS.md's rule that process cost never weighs in a recommendation (owner, 2026-10-27) made every recommendation ignore the cycles it started.

The session working 066 reported four more costs while 078's review ran:

- A fix round diffed the whole working tree against its base, so another session's landing in the shared checkout joined the round as though it were the review's fix; 066 held every landing for 078's whole loop, with its task 18 gated in its own clone. The session did not measure how long the hold lasted. It suggested scoping a round to the paths the review's own fixes touch, or setting apart changes that arrived by a commit the review did not make.

- macOS arm64 runners went unacquired on three consecutive attempts (run 41856373009 attempt 2, and 41862325548 attempts 1 and 2), and 41897995093 attempt 2 then passed on `80b43a11`. The one attempt timed held about 15 minutes before it was cancelled (41856373009 attempt 2, 15:36:28Z to 15:51:29Z). That is the CI provider's capacity, outside this framework.

Verification did not scale with the change either. This spec's first commit, `50a63f60`, was one 66-line markdown file. It still ran the whole CI surface in a fresh clone first: 33 steps, including release tests, release clippy and `cargo audit`, in 4½ minutes with the release build already cached (from the gate log's first and last timestamps). Reopens were the larger measure of the outer loop, and review deferral explained only part of them. Measured at `7b49fc80` over each `specs/NNN-*/spec.md`'s history (`git log --follow -p -G'^status:'`, counting a commit whose diff removed `status: done` and added `status: in-progress`; the `done` → `draft` back-edge is not counted, and the method read 0 for 078 and 6 for 058, including `f47d2b03`), there were 406 `done` → `in-progress` transitions, across 65 of 77 specs and in 306 commits, with a median of 3 per spec. The most reopened were 022 (67), 050 (28), 026 (25), 017 (15), 020 (13), 047 (12) and 036 (10). 147 of the 406 came from commits that reopened two or more specs, the largest reopening 14. By commit-subject type they were `feat` 83, `amend` 82, `fix` 81, `status` 54, `impl` 32, `chore` 28, `groom` 16, `sweep` 10, `docs` 9 and `spec` 4; `review` was not among those ten. A subject's type does not show the cause, since an `amend` may follow from a review. §spec-lifecycle reopens a `done` spec on any meaningful edit, AGENTS.md's prose-claim sweep rule makes a sweep into a `done` body one, and AGENTS.md's routing rule sends shared runtime work to 022, 066 and 067. One review run was expensive, and the owner paid in decisions. By the harness's usage on each agent's completion notice, 078's second review used 1,839,091 subagent tokens: 1,594,543 across the four agents running the passes, whose longest took 871 seconds running concurrently, and 244,548 for the one re-read, which took 645 seconds. The host's own tokens are not in that figure. Counted from that session's transcript, it put 22 decisions to the owner, 14 of them on the review, and the owner's later choice reversed 4 of those. The re-read's 7 prose findings were what its 244,548 tokens bought in this run, and the passes, not the re-read, found that run's code defects, such as the `start-fix-rounds --feature .` deletion; 078's Motivation records the other side, a re-read of 076 that found a MUST.

## Behavior

- **A spec's review and analysis converge.** Each reopening of a spec, one pass from `in-progress` to `done`, holds at most one `/{project}:review` run and one `/{project}:analyze` run, and a second of either only after the earlier run left a gating finding. A reopening that changed none of the spec's code owes an analysis and no review.
- **A run creates work only where it is owed.** A review or analysis run adds a task, scenario or body edit to the spec it runs on only to dispose of a gating finding; everything else it finds is fixed in the run or waived with a reason.
- **A run reads its own spec's work.** What a review or analysis reads, and what its fix rounds re-read, is the spec's own change, so another spec's commits in the same tree neither enter it nor wait on it. A later review in the same reopening starts where the earlier one ended.
- **A behavior is stated once.** Each primitive's or command's behavior has one canonical description, and every other place points at it, so a change edits one sentence rather than seven.
- **The bound is measured, not asserted.** The outcome this spec targets is computed from the repository's history by the method its Resolved Questions record, and the measurement reports each reopening over the bound, each it could not count, and how many `in-progress` specs have no unchecked task.

The first and last bullets are settled; each other is a direction the Open Questions below settle, none yet a design.

## Acceptance Criteria

AC1, AC4 and AC5 are settled; the others are candidates the Open Questions below settle:

- [ ] AC1: Each reopening of a spec (one pass from `in-progress` to `done`) that closes after this spec ships holds at most one `/{project}:review` run and one `/{project}:analyze` run, and a second of either only after the earlier run left a gating finding, measured by a method this spec records that reports each reopening over the bound, each it could not attribute, and the count of `in-progress` specs with no unchecked task.
- [ ] AC4: A reopening that changed none of the spec's code reaches `done` with an `/{project}:analyze` run and no `/{project}:review` run of its own.
- [ ] AC2: A review or analysis run adds a task, scenario or body edit to the spec it runs on only to dispose of a gating finding.
- [ ] AC3: A review's scope and its fix rounds' paths exclude a change another spec's commit made, and the record names any it excluded.
- [ ] AC5: A review's default window starts at the later of the spec's last reopen and its last review commit, except that a review recording a skipped pass or a budget stop does not move the start, and `--since` still overrides it.

## Open Questions

- What is a review's scope: every path the repository changed since the base, as 066's union defines it, or the spec's own change (its plan's files and the commits made for it), and how is a commit attributed to a spec? The same question covers a fix round's paths (078's `brief-re-read`, which diffs the whole working tree) and `diff-cross-spec`'s window.
- How are a run's findings dispositioned so that it creates no new cycle: no deferral into the spec under review except for a gating finding; an observation fixed in the run when mechanical, otherwise waived; an observation about another spec's code waived rather than deferred, as AGENTS.md already says for the pipeline's machinery? This touches the disposition rules of `058-findings-route-at-discovery` and `076-waived-and-deferred-findings-are-distinct-dispositions` and the review and analyze references.
- Which surface is canonical for a primitive's or command's behavior (the data model, the doc comment, the MCP description, the command, the reference), and are the others reduced to pointers, generated from it, or checked against it?
- Does AGENTS.md's rule that process cost never weighs in a recommendation (owner, 2026-10-27) stay, change to weigh the cycles an option starts, or go?
- What verification does a change need before and after its push? Does the shared-checkout commit procedure (AGENTS.md's gotcha on `017` task 45) keep its two gate runs, and does a pre-commit lint step come first? And do the local gate and the wait on CI scale with what the change touches, as the constitution's blast-radius rule asks, rather than running the whole CI surface for a spec?
- Where does each change land: in this spec, or as a scenario on the spec that owns the surface (`020-code-review`, `066-audit-records-and-the-completion-gate`, `022-deterministic-runtime`, `058-findings-route-at-discovery`, `076-waived-and-deferred-findings-are-distinct-dispositions`, `017-derive-dont-ask` for the pre-commit hook, the constitution), back-linking this spec under AGENTS.md's routing rule?
- Does 078's own loop change? Two gaps stand: a fix round's paths include other sessions' changes in a shared tree (above), and a review cut off between its first `write-review` and its last leaves a record without the loop fields, which gate check 12 passes as predating the loop (078's `review.md`, waived on 2026-11-04 under option B).
- What should reopen a `done` spec? §spec-lifecycle reopens one on any meaningful edit, and AGENTS.md's prose-claim sweep rule makes a sweep into a `done` body one, so a behavior change reopens every `done` spec that describes it. Does a correction to a `done` spec's prose still reopen it and stale its review, as 066's task 17 would for 078's data model, or does it take a lighter path? What does the reopen count in Motivation say the change should target?
- Is AGENTS.md's routing rule itself a cause? It sends shared runtime work to 022, 066 and 067, and 022 led the corpus with 67 reopens and 52 review runs. Should this spec's own changes land on those hubs, or should the rule change first?
- What may one review run cost in tokens, elapsed time and decisions put to the owner, and does the outcome target bound them? Which decisions can stop reaching the owner (`074-delegated-gates-leave-proposals-for-review` overlaps), and does §recommendations' one-at-a-time default stay for a run's observations?
- Does the fix-round loop earn its cost? Should re-reads be kept, cut, or limited to rounds that change code, given what they found in 076 and in 078's second review, as Motivation records both?
- Must this spec leave the pipeline simpler than it found it? After 058, 076 and 078 each added machinery, and with `AGENTS.md` and the constitution at their size ceilings, does each change retire at least as much as it adds, measured by a stated method?
- AC1 counts reopenings that close after this spec ships, so it cannot be verified when this spec itself reaches `done`. What does `done` require instead: the measurement working over the recorded baseline, or a stated number of later closings first, and where does a later miss go?

## Resolved Questions

- **Where does a re-review's window start by default?** At the later of the spec's last reopen and its last review commit (owner, 2026-11-04). A first run in a reopening still starts at the reopen, and a later run starts at the last review commit. A run with `skipped-passes` or a `budget-stop` does not move the start, so stopping early leaves nothing permanently unread, and `--since` still overrides it. Measured at `d62e72f3` over the 135 review runs recorded since 2026-10-01, 31 of them later runs in a reopening, counting the paths changed between the window's start and the commit the run reviewed, which is the window's size and not what the run read. Today's start at the last reopen (`framework/reference/review.md` §Window and coverage) totals 13,801 paths, with a median of 44 for a first run and 163 for a later one. The chosen start totals 11,979, 13% fewer, with medians of 44 and 130; both totals are over the 134 runs that could be measured. Rejected: the last review commit alone, 40,126 paths over the 125 runs with an earlier review and a median of 351 for a first run, since a spec's last review can be weeks older than its reopening. A later run's window is still a median of 130 paths, because the scope question above decides whose changes count.
- **What outcome does this spec target, and how is it measured?** Per reopening, one pass from `in-progress` to `done`: at most one review run and one analyze run, a second of either only after the earlier run left a gating finding, and a reopening that changed none of the spec's code owes an analysis and no review (owner, 2026-11-04). Counting per reopening replaces "after its last planned task", since 66 of the 123 reopenings below ticked no task, and the all-time per-spec counts in Motivation sum across reopenings. Measured at `d62e72f3` over the 123 reopenings of 56 specs that closed since 2026-10-01, delimited by each spec's `status:` history, which held 255 runs: a run is a distinct `last-run` value in the spec's committed `review.md` or `analysis.md` inside the reopening, so two runs in one commit count as one and an uncommitted run as none. "Changed no code" was measured as no task ticked and no commit naming the spec in its subject touching a path outside `specs/`, review, analyze and status commits aside: 55 of the 123, holding 95 runs (37.3%). That proxy misses another spec's commit that changed this spec's code and counts a commit that merely names the spec, so the scope question above settles the real test. The chosen target can remove at most 66 runs (25.9%). Rejected, each with the most runs it could remove: 2 and 2 (1 run, 0.4%), which 122 of the 123 already met, so it could barely fail; 1 and 1 with no rule for unchanged code (37, 14.5%), which 94 of the 123 met; the same with no run at all on an unchanged-code reopening (121, 47.5%), which drops analysis, the only check comparing those text edits with the plan and tasks; and a one-day limit on a spec with no unchecked task (0 directly), which depends on someone starting the runs and which 9 of the 10 such specs at `d62e72f3` exceeded. At least 74% of the runs remain, mostly the first runs each reopening owes, which the question on what reopens a `done` spec reaches: all 65 specs were `done` at `c7ff8379` (2026-10-17) and 37 were `in-progress` at `d62e72f3`, read from each spec's `status:`. The target does not measure a run's own cost, which the question on what one run may cost decides.
- **Is this work a new spec, or 078 renamed and broadened?** A new spec, and 078 closes on the inner-loop scope it shipped (owner, 2026-11-04). Three reasons decided it. Seven of the eight levers the Open Questions then named live in other specs' code (020, 066, 022, 058, 076, AGENTS.md, the constitution), and the constitution sends a concern shared across siblings to its own spec. AGENTS.md's routing rule has each owner's scenario back-link to a requiring spec, which 078 could not be while also owning its own loop. And 078's name and criteria truly describe the loop it delivered, which is what `done` claims. Rejected: renaming 078 and broadening it. That meant a sweep of 13 files and 46 lines, 078 back to `draft` and re-planned, and its `done` waiting on this whole design. Whether 078's own loop changes is an Open Question above, and a yes reopens 078 by the normal back-edge.
